The server's clock decides where the event stands, and what counts as your team's submission is the last push the platform received before the freeze. This page is the rule, written for the team that pushes.

## Four phases

| Phase       | From                            | Until         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Not started | the organizers set the schedule | the start     |
| Building    | the start                       | the build end |
| Grace       | the build end                   | the freeze    |
| Closed      | the freeze                      | for ever      |

The freeze is always the build end plus 15 minutes. Those 15 minutes are for pushing your final changes,
not for building more. At the freeze the intake closes: a push received from then on is recorded and never
counts.

The vote, when the organizers turn it on, has its own window, which opens at the freeze or later
([Decks and the community vote](/hackathon/concepts/decks-and-voting)).

## The countdown is the server's

The **Home** page shows the phase, a countdown to the next deadline and every deadline in the event's time
zone, and a small timer stays on every page. The countdown follows the server's clock, never your device's:
a changed device clock or a sleeping tab never extends the event. The page reads the status again every 10
seconds, after a reconnect and when the tab becomes visible again.

From the command line, `hackathon event status` prints the phase, the deadlines, the next deadline with the
time left, your role and your team.

## The notices

A notice appears 15, 5 and 1 minute before the build end, at the start of the grace, 5 and 1 minute before
the freeze, and at the freeze. If the organizers move the build end while the event runs, every participant
sees a notice with their reason. After a reconnect or a sleep, the dashboard shows the critical notices it
missed, once.

## What counts as your submission

Each team has one repository and one designated branch. What the team pushes there before the freeze is its
submission. At the freeze the platform keeps an immutable copy of that exact revision, and that copy is what
the decks and the vote are built from.

1. **The platform's receipt of the push is the evidence.** GitHub tells the platform about every push. The
   platform records the moment it received the push, on its own clock.
2. **The eligible submission is the last push of the designated branch received before the freeze.** Commit
   dates play no part; the receipt order does.
3. **A push received at or after the freeze is recorded and never selected.** It shows as late. It never
   replaces the selection.
4. **The platform captures the exact commit the push named.** It never reads a newer revision, and it never
   falls back to an older push.
5. **A push GitHub accepted just before the freeze, but which reached the platform after it, does not
   count.** Delivery can take a few seconds or more. Do not push in the last seconds.

A push to another branch, a deleted branch or a push from a repository that is not a team's is recorded for
the organizers and is never a candidate.

## Push early and verify

A repository that exists is not a submission, and a commit on your machine is not one either. What counts is
what the platform received:

1. Push to the designated branch. The setup and the Home page name it.
2. Open the Home page and read the last received revision on your team's submission card, with the time it
   was received. `hackathon submissions list` prints the same line.
3. Compare it with your last commit, `git rev-parse HEAD`. If they differ, or nothing was received, push
   again and check again. Ask an organizer if the revision still does not arrive.

Do this early in the build, and again before the freeze.

## After the freeze

The frozen copy does not change when the team pushes again, force-pushes, rewrites history or deletes the
branch: the team keeps its rights on the repository, and the copy is not in it. A team that pushed nothing
eligible is frozen on its starter, the revision captured when the repository was created, and is marked
`template only`.

You read your own team's submission and can download its frozen archive with
`hackathon submissions artifact <project>`. Another team's submission is not readable.
